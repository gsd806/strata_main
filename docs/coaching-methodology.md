# Personal training and calorie counting methodology

STRATA’s coaching workspace is a planning tool for adults, not a diagnosis, a measured metabolic test, or medical nutrition therapy. Every saved week includes the inputs, formula name, assumptions, product guardrails, and model versions behind its results.

## Energy estimates

Energy semantics are part of the saved coaching profile schema. A newly created profile, or an existing profile the member explicitly reviews and saves in Build 7.10, is written as version 3. Version 3 estimates total daily energy expenditure directly with the sex-specific 2023 Dietary Reference Intake Estimated Energy Requirement (EER) equations published by the National Academies and [Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/healthy-eating/dietary-reference-intakes/tables/equations-estimate-energy-requirement.html). Those equations combine age, height, weight, and one of four physical-activity categories and were developed from doubly labeled water evidence. They are a better starting point for maintenance than multiplying a resting estimate by the older generic `1.2–1.9` activity factors.

For version 3, the activity answer covers the whole day: work, walking, transport, chores, and training. STRATA therefore does not add generated-workout calories on top of EER; doing so would count the same activity twice. For adults, the published categories are inactive (`1.00 ≤ PAL < 1.53`), low active (`1.53 ≤ PAL < 1.68`), active (`1.68 ≤ PAL < 1.85`), and very active (`1.85 ≤ PAL < 2.50`). Health Canada's orientation examples begin with activities of daily living for inactive (for example, 30 minutes of walking plus 90 minutes of light-to-moderate household activity); low active adds 60–80 minutes of walking at 5–7 km/h; active and very active add progressively larger mixtures of walking, moderate cycling, tennis, and jogging. New version-3 profiles default to inactive, and a member should choose a higher category only when the typical whole day is a reasonable match. These examples are orientation, not a wearable measurement or a guarantee that the selected category matches the member's true physical-activity level.

For version 3, STRATA still calculates the sex-specific Mifflin–St Jeor resting estimate as a visible cross-check. The [original validation study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2305711/) derived it from indirect-calorimetry measurements in 498 healthy adults, but the resting result does not become the calorie target. An optional body-fat entry can add the Cunningham 1991 lean-mass resting-energy cross-check (`370 + 21.6 × fat-free mass in kg`); the [cited synthesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1957828/) proposed that equation. Consumer body-fat readings are often noisy, so entering one never silently changes the primary EER engine for a supported version-3 profile. The form calls the female/male selection an equation coefficient rather than gender identity because it selects one of the published equation branches.

Stored version-1 and version-2 profiles are an explicit compatibility path, not malformed version-3 profiles. They retain the exact Build 7.9 calculation and original activity meaning: Mifflin–St Jeor when body fat was absent, or the historical `370 + 21.6 × fat-free mass in kg` resting value when it was supplied, multiplied by the stored legacy `1.2–1.9` activity factor. Reading the profile, rolling into a new week, or deploying Build 7.10 never reinterprets that activity answer as a 2023 PAL category. Their interface state is labeled “Legacy profile,” and trend evidence cannot adjust their target. Only an explicit profile review and save opts into version 3 and deliberately regenerates the current week.

The published adult EER table starts at age 19. Existing version-1/version-2 profiles saved at age 18 or without an equation coefficient remain readable and regenerable on the compatibility path. New setup and explicit conversion to version 3 require age 19 or older and the published equation coefficient.

Every starting result is shown as an approximate target with a conservative planning range. That range is an application warning band, not a statistical confidence interval. EER is a population estimate; only repeated real-world trends can reveal whether it is systematically high or low for one member.

## Trend calibration

A member can optionally enter a morning body weight and mark a daily intake total complete. An unmarked day is kept in the diary but is not treated as a complete observation. Version-1/version-2 profiles may display the evidence counts but always apply a zero adjustment until explicit conversion to version 3. For a version-3 profile, STRATA looks back at exactly the 21 calendar dates before a new Monday and requires at least 18 explicitly complete calorie days, 12 valid morning-weight observations, and at least 14 calendar days between the first and last usable weight before it can apply a correction. The observations do not have to cover all 21 dates, so “trend” means a trend estimated from qualifying observations across at least that 14-day span inside the lookback, not a continuously observed 21-day trend. Future entries, the week being generated, incomplete intake days, and invalid measurements are excluded.

When enough evidence exists, STRATA uses a robust Theil–Sen weight trend and the average of the complete calorie totals to form a feedback estimate. The calculation uses the short-window energy-balance approximation `average intake − daily weight trend × 7,700 kcal/kg`; it is not a metabolic measurement and does not reproduce the full NIDDK model. Self-reported intake commonly misses food, and scale weight moves with hydration, glycogen, digestion, and measurement timing, so only 35% of the difference from the EER baseline is used. A weekly correction is rounded to 25 kcal and cannot exceed 150 kcal in either direction.

Calibration fails closed when the evidence is sparse or incomplete, the weight trend exceeds 1.5% of body weight per week, the feedback estimate falls outside 1,000–6,000 kcal/day, or it differs from baseline by more than 40%. The interface then stays at “Starting estimate” or “Calibrating,” explains what is missing, and makes no correction. An accepted result is labeled “Trend-informed,” shows the evidence cutoff and adjustment, and remains an estimate.

Every calibration design value—the 21-day lookback, 18- and 12-observation gates, 14-day minimum weight span, Theil–Sen and outlier handling, `7,700 kcal/kg` conversion, 35% shrinkage, 25-kcal rounding, ±150-kcal cap, 1.5%-per-week rejection, 1,000–6,000-kcal plausibility range, and 40% baseline-gap rejection—is an unvalidated STRATA product heuristic. Research on [energy-intake estimation from repeated body weight](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26040640/) informs the general feedback concept, but that study validated a full dynamic metabolism model over much longer intervals; it did not validate STRATA's 21-day calculation or thresholds. The review of [self-reported energy intake](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31920966/) supports the under-reporting caution, not the accuracy of an individual STRATA adjustment.

The current weekly snapshot stays fixed. Completing or editing a diary entry cannot move today's target; eligible evidence can affect a newly generated version-3 week on the next Monday. A deliberate legacy-profile review/save changes the profile to version 3 and regenerates the current week immediately, making the model transition explicit rather than allowing background activity to change the number.

Goal targets remain conservative:

- Gentle deficit: up to 10% below estimated maintenance.
- Moderate deficit: up to 15% below estimated maintenance, capped at 500 kcal per day.
- Gentle surplus: about 5% above estimated maintenance.
- Moderate surplus: about 7.5% above estimated maintenance, bounded to 100–250 kcal per day.
- No generated day can fall below 1,200 kcal. A deficit is not generated when current BMI is below 18.5, when the product's calorie floor cannot be maintained, or when a displayed 4-, 8-, or 12-week lower scenario would cross that BMI boundary. These are non-clinical product guardrails, not evidence that a generated target is safe or suitable for an individual. STRATA does not collect enough clinical information to screen pregnancy, breastfeeding, eating disorders, medical conditions, medications, growth, or a clinician's restriction; affected members must not use the deficit planner and should seek qualified care.

Steady, training-day zigzag, and flexible-day schedules redistribute the same seven-day calorie budget. If a requested variation would cross the calorie floor, STRATA falls back to a steady schedule and explains why. “Flexible day” is used instead of moral language such as “cheat day.”

Optional macro targets request a nominal protein rate of 1.6–2.0 g/kg, then cap protein at 30% of energy, set fat near 25% of energy, and assign remaining energy to carbohydrate. When the calorie cap binds, the generated protein grams can be lower than 1.6 g/kg; the displayed gram target, not the nominal rate, is the actual output. The requested exercise-protein range follows the [International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/); the results remain planning targets, not clinical prescriptions.

## Remaining-day food options

The daily intake log can turn the unconsumed portion of that day's calorie target—and, when enabled, protein, carbohydrate, and fat targets—into up to three meal-menu options. The planner uses the member's saved dietary pattern, dietary requirements, supported allergen selections, favorite-food categories, preferred 1–6 meals per day, and optional daily food budget. Meal count is a scheduling preference, not a claim that one eating frequency is healthier than another.

The server derives these options when the member requests them; it does not treat a suggestion as food eaten and does not add it to the daily intake log. It subtracts the saved intake values from that day's generated target, estimates one to the preferred daily meal count from the fraction of calories still remaining, divides the balance across those remaining meals, and bounds each catalog portion from 0.25 to 3.5 servings. This is a presentation heuristic, not an inference about meals already eaten. When macro targets are enabled and all three consumed macros are entered, ranking considers the remaining macro proportions as well as calories. If a calorie entry omits those optional macros, STRATA switches that request to calorie-only matching rather than inventing a macro remainder.

A menu receives `ready` status only when three distinct alternatives pass every hard filter and each is within the greater of 100 kcal or 10% of the remaining calorie target. A result outside that tolerance is labeled `limited`, keeps its actual difference visible, and asks the member to review portions manually rather than presenting a close match.

Constraints have a deliberate order:

1. Saved supported allergens, dietary pattern, and gluten-free or dairy-free requirements filter the catalog before any ranking occurs.
2. If an allergy is outside the supported tags or the member is unsure, STRATA withholds automatic options and asks for manual review; it does not guess from free text.
3. Favorite-food matches and the optional USD budget affect ordering only. They never reintroduce a meal removed by an allergy or dietary rule.
4. If no meal passes every hard filter, the result explains that no compatible catalog option was found rather than weakening a constraint.

STRATA's supported allergen checklist follows the US Food and Drug Administration's nine major food-allergen categories: milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy, and sesame. That list is not exhaustive, and an ingredient match cannot establish that a packaged product, kitchen, or serving is free from cross-contact. Members must still inspect current labels and preparation conditions. See the FDA's [food-allergy overview](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) and [consumer label guidance](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/have-food-allergies-read-label).

The bundled meal catalog uses approximate household ingredient amounts and rounded STRATA editorial recipe estimates informed by generic values in [USDA FoodData Central](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/). FoodData Central is the reference source, not a live lookup, item-by-item audit trail, or guarantee about a branded product. The listed amounts define one base meal; the displayed multiplier scales that combination. Actual measurement, preparation, substitutions, and manufacturer formulations can change calories and macros. The catalog fingerprint returned with a result makes the exact bundled catalog revision reviewable and keeps same-input suggestions deterministic.

Costs are rough US-dollar ingredient estimates, not current local prices. The daily budget creates a visible comparison and a ranking penalty for options that exceed it; it is not a hard promise that a menu can be purchased for that amount. The daily intake log does not collect actual spending, so the comparison uses the complete entered daily budget rather than pretending to know what has already been spent. Brand, store, country, tax, season, package size, delivery, leftovers, and waste can change actual cost. The planner follows general budget principles such as comparing unit prices, using durable staples, and planning leftovers from the USDA MyPlate guidance on [eating healthy on a budget](https://www.myplate.gov/sites/default/files/2024-06/TipSheet-23-Eat-Healthy-On-A-Budget.pdf) and [budget-friendly weekly meals](https://www.myplate.gov/eathealthy/budget/budget-weekly-meals), but it does not receive live grocery inventory or pricing.

## Weight scenarios

The 4-, 8-, and 12-week values use a deliberately simplified dynamic-response approximation inspired by the research behind the [NIDDK Body Weight Planner](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/research-funding/at-niddk/labs-branches/laboratory-biological-modeling/integrative-physiology-section/research/body-weight-planner) and [Hall and colleagues’ dynamic model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880593/). For a selected daily calorie difference, STRATA estimates pounds changed as `daily kcal difference ÷ 10 × (1 − 2^(−days/365))`, then converts that value to kilograms. Its displayed band is the center plus or minus the greater of 0.5 kg or 40% of the modeled change. That band construction is an unvalidated STRATA display heuristic, not statistical uncertainty, a confidence interval, or a clinically reviewed safety boundary. STRATA does not claim to reproduce the full NIDDK model. Values are rounded and described as scenarios rather than promises. Hydration, glycogen, digestion, adherence, medication, health status, muscle gain, and individual metabolism can materially change scale weight.

## Weekly training generation

The weekly plan is deterministic. Its key combines the canonical coaching profile—including its energy-semantics version—and revision, Monday date, exercise and meal catalog fingerprints, workout-generator version, energy-model version, and a fingerprint of the bounded pre-week calibration evidence. Build 7.10 identifies this implementation as `energy-planning-v2`; that identifier invalidates incompatible generated snapshots but does not upgrade a version-1/version-2 profile or change its semantics. The plan remains stable within a week, then rotates on the next Monday in the member’s saved time zone. A profile edit creates a new current-week plan immediately; converting version 1 or 2 to version 3 is therefore a deliberate, reviewable model change.

Exercise selection uses the member’s training days, session duration, experience, equipment, saved movement limitations, and entered exercise capabilities. A capability is context—not a verified one-repetition maximum. If no complete session can satisfy the constraints, STRATA asks the member to review them instead of silently choosing an excluded movement.

Plans progress repetitions before load. Once every set reaches the top of its repetition range with controlled form on two comparable sessions, the workspace suggests a small load increase. This follows the progression principles in the [American College of Sports Medicine position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/). The workspace also explains that general US guidance recommends muscle-strengthening work on at least two days per week while still allowing a one-day starting plan; the [Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/current-guidelines) emphasize gradual progression for inactive adults.

## Safety and privacy boundaries

- Version-3 estimates are for adults ages 19–80. Previously saved version-1/version-2 profiles, including age-18 records, remain readable through the clearly labeled legacy calculation until explicitly reviewed and saved.
- Not designed for pregnancy, eating-disorder treatment, minors, medical nutrition therapy, or injury-specific programming.
- Pain is a reason to stop or modify a movement and seek qualified care, not a signal to train through it.
- Meal options are planning ideas, not prescriptions, allergy certification, therapeutic diets, verified nutrition measurements, or purchase-price guarantees.
- Height, profile weight, optional morning weights, body-fat input, exercise capabilities, saved dietary pattern, allergies and uncertainty note, dietary requirements, favorite-food categories, meal count, budget, generated weeks, and intake logs are private account data. The canonical profile is also embedded in the private current-week snapshot so its generation inputs remain reviewable. Coaching APIs require an authenticated, currently entitled Strata+ session. Mutations require same-origin and CSRF validation. Account deletion removes coaching data through database cascades, and account export includes the profile, weekly snapshots, and daily evidence with the member’s other private records.
